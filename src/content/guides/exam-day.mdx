## The format

| | AIF-C01 |
|---|---|
| Questions | {EXAM_FORMAT.items}: {EXAM_FORMAT.scoredItems} scored and {EXAM_FORMAT.unscoredItems} unscored |
| Time | {EXAM_FORMAT.minutes} minutes |
| Result | pass or fail, with a scaled score from {EXAM_FORMAT.scaleMin} to {EXAM_FORMAT.scaleMax}; you pass at **{EXAM_FORMAT.passScore}** |
| Question types | multiple choice (one right answer out of four) and multiple response (two or more right answers out of five or more) |
| Price | 100 USD (AWS lists other currencies on its exam pricing page) |
| Where | a Pearson VUE test centre, or online with a proctor |
| Valid for | 3 years |

- **The 15 unscored questions are not marked.** AWS uses them to try out future questions. You can't tell which ones they are, so give every question the same effort.
- **The score is compensatory.** You don't need to pass each domain, only the exam as a whole. A weak domain can be made up elsewhere, but domain 3 (28%) is the biggest, so it's the worst one to be weak in.
- **Unanswered questions are scored as wrong, and there is no penalty for guessing.** Never leave a question blank.
- For multiple-response questions, the question says how many answers to pick, for example "(Select TWO.)". Read that before you read the options.
- Exam guide v{GUIDE.version} lists only the two types above. The extended kinds in this app (ordering, matching and case studies) are optional extra practice: they test the same knowledge from another angle. Switch them off on the [progress page](/progress) if you want practice that looks only like the guide.

## Pacing

{EXAM_FORMAT.minutes} minutes for {EXAM_FORMAT.items} questions is about **1 minute 23 seconds per question**. Aim for about 1 minute 15 seconds, which leaves around 9 minutes at the end for questions you want to look at again.

| By minute | you should be past question |
|---|---|
| 20 | 16 |
| 40 | 32 |
| 60 | 48 |
| 81 | 65, then go back to the ones you were unsure of |

- **If a question takes more than 2 minutes, pick your best answer, note it for review and move on.** A hard question is worth no more than an easy one.
- Most AIF-C01 questions are short scenarios at Cairnway-like companies. They test whether you can match a need to a concept or a service, not whether you remember numbers.
- The [mock exam](/mock) uses the real length and time limit. Take at least two under exam conditions: no lessons, no notes, phone away.

## Reading a scenario question

1. **Read the last sentence first.** It tells you what is being asked: a service, a technique, a risk, a metric.
2. **Underline the constraint.** Most wrong options are good ideas that miss one word in the question. Typical constraints:

| The question says | Usually points to |
|---|---|
| "least operational overhead", "fully managed", "no ML expertise" | a managed AI service or Amazon Bedrock, not training your own model on SageMaker AI |
| "use the company's own, up-to-date documents" | retrieval augmented generation (RAG), for example Bedrock Knowledge Bases, not fine-tuning |
| "change the model's style or teach it a specialised task with labelled examples" | fine-tuning |
| "lowest cost" for a model that is good enough | the smallest model that meets the need, prompt engineering before RAG before fine-tuning, batch inference when answers can wait |
| "block harmful content", "deny a topic", "mask PII in prompts and answers" | Amazon Bedrock Guardrails |
| "who called which API, and when" | AWS CloudTrail |
| "find PII in S3 buckets" | Amazon Macie |
| "AWS's compliance reports" (SOC, ISO) | AWS Artifact |
| "explain a prediction", "detect bias in data" | SageMaker Clarify |
| "send low-confidence predictions to a person" | Amazon Augmented AI (A2I) |
| "document a model's intended use and risks" | SageMaker Model Cards |
| "keep traffic to Bedrock off the internet" | AWS PrivateLink (VPC endpoints) |

3. **Eliminate before you choose.** Cross out options that are out of scope for the question (a speech service for a text problem), that do more than asked (training a model when a managed service exists), or that are a different stage of the lifecycle.
4. **Watch for absolutes.** Options with "always", "guarantees" or "eliminates" are usually wrong: no technique removes hallucinations or bias completely.
5. The [cheat sheet](/guides/cheat-sheet) collects the facts these questions rely on, domain by domain.

## What the exam doesn't test

## Before the day

- **Book early, and know the 24-hour rule.** You can reschedule or cancel up to 24 hours before your appointment, and reschedule at most twice. Inside those 24 hours you can't, and missing the exam forfeits the fee.
- **Bring the right ID.** AWS asks for valid government-issued ID, and a second form of ID as well. The rules differ between online and test-centre exams, so read them on the policies page for your option.
- **Not a native English speaker?** You can request "ESL +30", 30 extra minutes on exams taken in English. Request it once in your AWS Certification account, under Request Exam Accommodations, **before you register**; it then applies to every future exam. With it you have about 1 minute 50 seconds per question.
- **Taking it online?** You'll need to talk to the proctor to complete the appointment. Test your computer and room against Pearson VUE's online requirements well before the day.
- **Results** arrive within 5 business days in your AWS Certification account, under Exam History.

## The day before

- Read the cheat sheet once. Don't start new topics.
- Review your weak tasks on the [progress page](/progress), and do one short [weak-spot practice](/practice?weak=1) set, not a full mock.
- Sleep. A rested brain reads scenarios more carefully than a crammed one.
