## Before you start

- An AWS account and an IAM user or role that may use Amazon Bedrock, Amazon S3 and IAM, in **US East (N. Virginia)**.
- Cairnway wants short summaries of long product reviews. Before it picks a model, it wants evidence: how close are the summaries to good ones, do they hold up when the input has typos, and are they ever toxic? That's what an **automatic** evaluation measures.

## Steps

### Create an S3 bucket for the results

Open **Amazon S3** and create a general purpose bucket in N. Virginia, for example `cairnway-eval-` followed by random digits. The job writes its results there. (Only human-based jobs need extra CORS settings on the bucket; automatic jobs don't.)

### Start an automatic evaluation

Open **Amazon Bedrock**, choose **Model evaluation** in the navigation pane, and in the **Build an evaluation** card, under **Automatic**, choose **Create automatic evaluation**. Name the job `cairnway-summaries` and, under **Models**, choose a small model such as **Amazon Nova Micro**.

### Choose the task type and the metrics

Set **Task type** to **Text summarization**. Under **Metrics and datasets**, keep the built-in datasets AWS suggests for each metric:

- **Accuracy**: how close each summary is to a reference summary, measured with **BERTScore**.
- **Robustness**: how much the output changes when each prompt is slightly changed, for example with typos, all lower case or extra spaces. A low robustness score is good.
- **Toxicity**: whether the output contains harmful language.

Each built-in dataset is based on an open-source dataset, randomly cut down to 100 prompts.

### Set the results location and the service role

Under **Evaluation results**, choose **Browse S3** and pick your bucket. Under **Amazon Bedrock IAM role**, choose **Create a new role**. Then choose **Create**. The job runs for a while; refresh the list until its status is **Completed**.

### Read the report card

Open the finished job to see its report card: one score per metric, with examples. Ask yourself: is the accuracy good enough for Cairnway, and does the output stay stable when the input is messy? To compare models, you would run the same job for a second model and compare the cards.

## What this shows for the exam

- **Amazon Bedrock Model Evaluation** is the managed way to compare foundation models on your task: **automatic** (built-in metrics and datasets), **human-based** (your own reviewers, for tone or brand voice), **LLM-as-a-judge**, and **RAG** evaluations of knowledge bases.
- **BERTScore** compares meaning, so a good paraphrase still scores well. **ROUGE** (summaries) and **BLEU** (translation) count overlapping words instead.
- **Benchmark datasets** give fast, repeatable, comparable scores; they don't tell you whether the model meets a **business** goal.

## Teardown

### Delete the evaluation job

In **Model evaluation**, select the job in the **Model Evaluation Jobs** card and choose **Delete**. This removes the job from Bedrock, but the results stay in your S3 bucket.

### Empty and delete the results bucket

In **Amazon S3**, empty the `cairnway-eval-…` bucket, then delete it.

### Delete the service role

Open **IAM**, choose **Roles**, find the role the job created (its name is shown on the job's details page), choose **Delete**, enter the role name and confirm.
